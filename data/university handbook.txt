UNIVERSITY ACADEMIC HANDBOOK 2025-2026

SECTION 1: ADMISSIONS AND ENROLLMENT

1.1 Undergraduate Admission Requirements
Prospective undergraduate students must have a high school diploma or equivalent with a minimum GPA of 2.5 on a 4.0 scale. International students must demonstrate English proficiency through TOEFL (minimum score 79) or IELTS (minimum score 6.5). The application deadline for Fall semester is June 15th, and for Spring semester is November 1st.

1.2 Graduate Admission Requirements
Graduate programs require a bachelor's degree from an accredited institution with a minimum GPA of 3.0. Applicants must submit two letters of recommendation, a statement of purpose, and official transcripts. GRE scores are required for STEM programs with a minimum of 300 combined score. Application deadline for graduate programs is March 31st for Fall admission.

SECTION 2: TUITION AND FINANCIAL AID

2.1 Tuition Costs for 2025-2026
Undergraduate tuition is $350 per credit hour for in-state students and $850 per credit hour for out-of-state students. Graduate tuition is $500 per credit hour for in-state and $1,100 for out-of-state students. Full-time undergraduate students (12-18 credits) pay a flat rate of $4,200 per semester for in-state residents.

2.2 Financial Aid Options
The university offers need-based grants, merit scholarships, work-study programs, and federal student loans. The FAFSA deadline is March 1st for priority consideration. Presidential Merit Scholarships range from $2,000 to $10,000 annually for students with a high school GPA of 3.5 or higher and SAT scores above 1200.

SECTION 3: ACADEMIC POLICIES

3.1 Grading System
The university uses a 4.0 grade point average scale. Letter grades convert as follows: A (4.0) for 90-100%, B (3.0) for 80-89%, C (2.0) for 70-79%, D (1.0) for 60-69%, and F (0.0) for below 60%. A minimum cumulative GPA of 2.0 is required for good academic standing.

3.2 Withdrawal and Incomplete Policy
Students may withdraw from a course without penalty before the end of the second week of classes. Withdrawal with a "W" grade is permitted until the tenth week. An incomplete grade ("I") may be granted only for medical or family emergencies with documentation. Incomplete grades must be completed within 30 days of the following semester.

SECTION 4: STUDENT SERVICES

4.1 Library Services
The main library is open Monday-Friday 8 AM to midnight, Saturday 10 AM to 8 PM, and Sunday 12 PM to 10 PM. Students can borrow up to 50 books for 30 days with one 15-day renewal. The library offers 24/7 online access to 200+ research databases, e-books, and academic journals.

4.2 Academic Advising
Each student is assigned a faculty advisor in their major department. Advising appointments are required before course registration each semester. The Academic Advising Center is located in Room 101 of Student Services Building, open Monday-Friday 9 AM to 5 PM.

SECTION 5: CAMPUS SAFETY

5.1 Campus Police and Emergency Procedures
The campus police department operates 24/7 at (555) 123-4567. Emergency blue light phones are located throughout campus. In case of emergency, dial 911 from any campus phone. The university sends emergency alerts via text message to all enrolled students.

SECTION 6: GRADUATION REQUIREMENTS

6.1 Bachelor's Degree Requirements
To earn a bachelor's degree, students must complete a minimum of 120 credit hours with a cumulative GPA of 2.0 or higher. Requirements include 42 credit hours of general education, 45-60 credit hours in the major, and elective courses. Students must also complete a capstone project or comprehensive exam in their final year.
